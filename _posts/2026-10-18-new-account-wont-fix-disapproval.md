A small tourism advertiser in Sri Lanka had a Search campaign that kept being disapproved. Three options were on the table: fix the ad copy, launch a new campaign, or open a new ad account.

## Why two of those options are traps

Recreating the same ads in a new campaign or account to get around a review is what Google's **Circumventing systems** policy is designed to catch. That's a far more serious violation than one disapproved ad, and it can affect accounts beyond the one you started with.

## Finding the actual cause

The disapproval sat under the **Government documents and services** policy. Google updated it and began enforcing the stricter version on **5 October 2026** ([Google policy notice](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/17260489?hl=en)). Under it, only providers explicitly authorised by the relevant government can run ads promoting direct acquisition of covered documents and services.

My working hypothesis: the site mentioned arranging driving permits for visitors, which could fall in scope even though permits were a side offer to the main service.

## The two legitimate paths

- **If the site arranges permits:** remove that offer from ads and landing pages, or meet the authorisation criteria
- **If the site only informs** visitors about permits: request a review explaining that, through Policy manager

Which one applies depends on what the business actually does. That's a question for the advertiser, not something to assume.

## The lesson

When Google's policy changes, accounts that were fine yesterday get flagged today. Check the policy change log before you check the ad copy.
