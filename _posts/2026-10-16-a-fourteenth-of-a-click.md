Shared budgets let several campaigns draw from one pot. In the right setup they're efficient. In the account I audited, they had become a quiet way to stop campaigns running.

## The maths

One shared budget was split across up to ten campaigns. Each campaign's effective share worked out to roughly **one-fourteenth of the cost of a single click** in its target market.

A campaign that can't afford one click a day can't gather data, and a campaign that can't gather data can't be optimised by Smart Bidding.

## What else was sitting in the structure

- Eleven **empty** shared budgets left behind after an earlier restructure, together holding allocation equal to roughly **a third** of the account's actual average daily spend
- A duplicate shared budget
- One budget with eleven campaigns attached delivering **zero** impressions
- Close to **28%** of total spend running behind creative that had policy issues

## A correction worth admitting

Early on, I inferred from the data that these shared budgets weren't really pooling. A screenshot of the Shared Budgets screen proved me wrong: they were live and active. That made the finding worse, not better. I corrected the analysis and kept the correction visible in the working notes. A wrong inference caught by evidence is part of the process, not something to hide.

## The useful conclusion

The client was considering new channel investment. The audit showed that money was already approved, just trapped in a structure that couldn't spend it. Restructuring the budgets could fund the new work without a single extra dollar of approval.
